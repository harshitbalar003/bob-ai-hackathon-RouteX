# Data Sources

> **Rubric asset.** This document states, for every dataset the project uses:
> whether it is synthetic, what reference data is used, and where that reference
> data came from. Required by the hackathon rules (no client data, no personal
> information, no social-media-sourced data).

---

## 1. Shipments (120 rows)

**Synthetic.** All shipment records — IDs, shipper names, consignee names, cargo
descriptions, leg schedules, and financial values — are generated programmatically
by `app/seed/generate.py` using a fixed random seed. No real consignee names,
no real shipment references, no real financial data. Shipper and consignee names
are invented (e.g. "Pharmalink GmbH", "NovaCold Logistics") — they do not
correspond to real companies.

Six cold-chain shipments (`shp-101` to `shp-106`) are scripted exactly in
`app/seed/scenarios/storm_rotterdam.yaml` to produce deterministic telemetry
demonstration cases.

---

## 2. Disruptions (3 rows)

**Synthetic with reference geography.** The three disruption records are
scripted in `storm_rotterdam.yaml`. Their descriptions are invented for the
demonstration; they do not represent real historical incidents.

The **geographic coordinates** of disruption areas use publicly published
reference data:

| Disruption | Geographic reference | Source |
|---|---|---|
| dis-001 Typhoon Krathon | Shanghai Yangshan port coordinates (31.35°N, 121.87°E) | UN/LOCODE public directory; JTWC track archive (general reference) |
| dis-002 Rotterdam strike | Rotterdam port polygon (51.86–51.97°N, 3.97–4.25°E) | UN/LOCODE public directory; OpenStreetMap (public domain) |
| dis-003 A2 corridor | Stuttgart coordinates (48.78°N, 9.18°E) | UN/LOCODE public directory |

UN/LOCODE is published by the United Nations Centre for Trade Facilitation and
Electronic Business (UN/CEFACT): https://www.unece.org/cefact/locode/service/location.html

---

## 3. Network Nodes (~25 rows)

**Reference data — publicly available.** Port and airport codes are drawn from
two published reference datasets:

| Code type | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| UN/LOCODE (5-char, e.g. `CNSGH`) | UN/CEFACT LOCODE directory, 2024 edition | Public domain — published by the United Nations |
| IATA airport codes (3-char, e.g. `FRA`) | IATA Airport Code Reference, published list | Public domain identifiers (codes themselves are not IP-protected) |

The **coordinates** (lat/lng) associated with each node are derived from the
UN/LOCODE directory coordinates column where available, supplemented by
OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL licence) for inland hubs
and border crossings not in UN/LOCODE.

No private port authority data, no AIS vessel tracking, no operator-provided
berth schedules.

---

## 4. Lanes

**Synthetic.** Lane records (transit hours, cost per unit, capacity, reefer
capability) are generated by `app/seed/generate.py`. Transit hours are
estimated from great-circle distance and typical carrier schedule data
(published in freely available carrier service bulletins and academic literature
on maritime networks); they are not taken from any proprietary dataset.

Cost-per-unit values are invented and normalised to a plausible range — they are
not real tariffs.

---

## 5. Fleet Assets (40 rows)

**Synthetic.** Asset IDs, types, statuses, capacities, and utilisation figures
are generated by `app/seed/generate.py`. Location coordinates are placed at
or near the network nodes defined above. No real fleet ownership records, no
real vehicle identification numbers, no real GPS telemetry.

---

## 6. Sensor Readings (~4,600 rows per cold-chain shipment, ~27,000 total)

**Synthetic.** All temperature and humidity readings are generated by a
first-order thermal model in `app/seed/telemetry.py`. The model is parameterised
by physically plausible constants (thermal time constant, cooling rate, door-open
gain) drawn from the open literature:

| Parameter | Source |
|---|---|
| Thermal time constant (reefer container) | K. Tassou et al., "A review of the energy consumption of refrigerated food transport", Applied Thermal Engineering 2009 — general order-of-magnitude reference |
| MKT formula and constants | USP General Chapter <1079> "Good Storage and Distribution Practices", Table 1 worked example. Freely available on USP.org. |
| GDP temperature thresholds (2–8 °C) | EU GDP Guidelines (2013/C 343/01), published in the Official Journal of the European Union. Freely available. |
| WHO cold chain thresholds | WHO Technical Report Series No. 961 (2011), Annex 9. Freely available on who.int. |

No real sensor hardware data, no real reefer unit logs, no data from any
real shipment.

---

## 7. Excursions (3 scripted rows)

**Synthetic — derived from the telemetry model.** The three excursion records
(`exc-001`, `exc-002`, `exc-003`) are the output of running the cold-chain
engine (`app/engines/cold_chain.py`) against the synthetic sensor readings.
They are stored in the database and exported to fixtures by `app/export_fixtures.py`.

---

## 8. Reroute Options (8 rows)

**Synthetic.** Generated by `app/engines/rerouting.py` using the synthetic
network graph. No real carrier booking data, no real freight quotes.

---

## 9. Regulatory Rule Packs (YAML files)

**Derived from published regulatory text.** The YAML files in `app/rules/`
encode threshold values and classification rules cited against:

| File | Source regulation | URL |
|---|---|---|
| `gdp.yaml` | EU Good Distribution Practice Guidelines (2013/C 343/01) | https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:C:2013:343:TOC |
| `who_pqs.yaml` | WHO TRS 961 Annex 9 (2011) | https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-TRS-961 |
| `usp_1079.yaml` | USP General Chapter <1079> | https://www.usp.org/sites/default/files/usp/document/our-work/1079_storageDistribution.pdf |
| `fsma.yaml` | FDA FSMA Final Rule 21 CFR Part 1, Subpart O | https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-sanitary-transportation-human-and-animal-food |

The threshold values are re-stated from these public documents. No proprietary
pharmaceutical compliance software was used or referenced.
